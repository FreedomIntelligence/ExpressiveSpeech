# Prosody Richness Labeling Standards

### General Rules:
- Labeling is conducted across 3 dimensions: **Pitch Variation**, **Volume Variation**, and **Tempo Variation**.
- **Crucial Note:** All dimensions measure the degree of **variation/change**, not absolute values.
- Each dimension is scored on a 3-level scale:
    - **0**: No variation at all
    - **1**: Some variation
    - **2**: Significant variation

---

## 1. Pitch Variation (The "Tune" of the voice)
**Analogy:** Think of the voice as a line—is it a flat, horizontal line, or a rolling mountain range?

- **0 Points: Robotic**
  - **Feeling:** The voice stays on almost the same note from beginning to end; it is as flat as a straight line.
  - **Sounds like:** Early versions of Siri, GPS navigation, or someone reading a list of numbers (e.g., "1234567") with zero emotion.
  - **Self-Check:** "Is this voice so monotonous it's making me sleepy?"
- **1 Point: Standard Speech / Reading Aloud**
  - **Feeling:** There are highs and lows, but they are very "orderly." For example, the pitch drops at the end of a sentence or rises when asking a question.
  - **Sounds like:** A news anchor, a teacher reading a textbook, or how you and I talk in a normal, everyday conversation.
  - **Self-Check:** "Does this sound like a normal human? Yes, it's normal, but nothing special."
- **2 Points: Storyteller / Actor**
  - **Feeling:** The pitch changes frequently and dramatically; it is highly expressive and infectious.
  - **Sounds like:** Professional storytelling, a dramatic performance, or the way someone speaks when they are extremely excited or surprised.
  - **Self-Check:** "Wow, this is so expressive! I can almost see a 'picture' just by listening to the tone."

## 2. Volume Variation (The "Strength" of the voice)
**Analogy:** Does the speaker use the same amount of "force" throughout, or is it sometimes light and sometimes heavy?

- **0 Points: No Emphasis**
  - **Feeling:** Every word is spoken with the same amount of force; you can't tell which word is the most important.
  - **Sounds like:** Again, the "robotic" style where every syllable is spat out at the exact same volume.
  - **Self-Check:** "They said a lot, but I have no idea what the 'key point' was."
- **1 Point: Natural Emphasis (Noticeable but not exaggerated)**
  - **Feeling:** You can hear that certain words are spoken with more force, helping you grasp the core meaning of the sentence.
  - **Sounds like:** A teacher emphasizing keywords, e.g., "The **key** to this problem is..."
  - **Self-Check:** "Can I tell which word the speaker is trying to highlight?"
- **2 Points: Performance-Level Dynamics**
  - **Feeling:** The volume is used masterfully—sudden loud bursts for emphasis followed by soft, quiet whispers.
  - **Sounds like:** A public speaker raising their voice to grab attention, or lowering it to a whisper to create suspense.
  - **Self-Check:** "Is the speaker using 'loudness' and 'softness' to manipulate my emotions?"

## 3. Tempo Variation (The "Speed and Pausing")
**Analogy:** Is the voice moving at a constant speed like a machine, or is it like a driver who speeds up, slows down, and stops strategically?

- **0 Points: Like a Metronome**
  - **Feeling:** The speed is unchanging; the intervals between words are exactly the same with no pauses.
  - **Sounds like:** Words "popping out" one by one in a mechanical, rigid rhythm.
  - **Self-Check:** "Is the rhythm as stiff and predictable as a machine?"
- **1 Point: Following the Traffic Rules**
  - **Feeling:** The speed is mostly stable, but the speaker "stops" at commas and periods. Pauses mainly occur where they are grammatically expected.
  - **Sounds like:** Most people reading a paragraph—fluent, but without any rhythmic "flair."
  - **Self-Check:** "Does the speaker only pause when there is a punctuation mark?"
- **2 Points: Like a Race Car Driver**
  - **Feeling:** A mix of fast and slow speeds with perfectly timed pauses. Sometimes they speak very quickly, then suddenly stop to catch your attention.
  - **Sounds like:** A comedian's "rapid-fire" delivery or the "dramatic pause" a stand-up comic uses before a punchline.
  - **Self-Check:** "Does the variation in speed and pausing make the sentence more interesting?"

### Important Notes:
1. **Focus on Acoustics Only:** Base your score strictly on the sound qualities. Do not let the meaning (semantics) of the text influence your judgment.
2. **Independent Scoring:** Evaluate each dimension separately. Be especially careful not to confuse **Pitch** (the musical note) with **Volume** (the physical force/loudness).
