# Human-likeness Labeling Standards

The purpose of this document is to provide a standardized framework for grading the "Human-likeness" of speech across various datasets.

## Definition of Human-likeness
**Core Question:** Does the audio sound like a "living, breathing human being" is speaking?

To determine this, we focus on three key indicators:
- **Physiological Traces:** Does the audio include natural breathing and inhalations at appropriate moments? Are there subtle, unavoidable physiological noises (e.g., slight lip smacks or dental sounds) that enhance the sense of reality?
- **Cognitive Footprints:** Does the speech include natural hesitation markers that reflect a thinking process (e.g., "um...", "uh...", "well...")?
- **Spontaneous Reactions:** Are non-verbal sounds—such as natural, unforced laughter, sighs, or sharp intakes of breath—integrated based on the content and emotional context?

**Note:** The primary emphasis for this labeling task is on **Physiological Traces**.

## Grading Standards

### Level-1: Very Low Human-likeness
The voice lacks any "breath of life." it is stiff, formulaic, and contains no natural breathing or airflow variations. It sounds purely mechanical—a textbook robot.
- **Typical Example:** Low-quality TTS (Text-to-Speech) audio that is dull and monotonous.

### Level-3: Moderate Human-likeness
